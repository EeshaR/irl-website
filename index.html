IRL

When you're physically at the same café, bar, coworking space, or event, IRL is a mutual-consent way to signal interest—so you both opt in before full profiles are exchanged. Discovery is anchored to venues in the United States; there's intentionally no open-ended messaging in the product.

How it works

You join supported venues explicitly, optionally turn discoverability on, and browse anonymous-facing cards drawn from prompts you choose to fill in. Until both sides agree through the in-app flow, richer identity stays private. Turning discoverability off, leaving a venue, or ending a session resets what others can reasonably infer about your presence—as described in-product.

Verification & control. A government-ID flow (paired with permissions for camera—and photo library only if you pick images from Photos) raises the likelihood that profiles map to identifiable adults—but it is not flawless. You decide when you are discoverable. None of this replaces sober judgment offline (Terms).

Data footprint (summary). We collect roughly what ships today: signup credentials, voluntary profile inputs, verification captures through the granted camera/library permissions, coarse location while you actively use discovery to infer venue presence, structured activity inside the Service, optional notifications your phone shows with your consent, plus ordinary server/network metadata tied to authenticated API calls—and not much else by design (Privacy Policy lists categories precisely).

Offline responsibility. IRL connects digital signals to real gatherings; we do not supervise, insure, vet every interaction, guarantee safety outcomes, assume liability for people you encounter, conversations you have offline, harassment off-platform, intoxication/incidents at venues, or anything analogous. See Terms.

Headquarters reachability for support, moderation handoffs, deletion, everything else: admin@irlapp.org.
